Oct 20, 2026 · @Andreas

The brand is OOF!, a stats-based F1 parody account, with the exclamation mark shaped like a "1".

- Logo: the O's as tyres (soft red, medium yellow, hard white), one worn or flat; the "!" has the angled flag of a "1", its dot a wheel nut or small tyre. Cardboard and tape as texture, not as extra elements.
- Trademark: no "F1" or "Formula 1" in handles or domain, no official logos, footage, driver photos or team radio audio.

- Project shorthand: "oof" for everything technical: the GitHub repo, the local folder, the database and the local domain oof.test.

## Format

Drivers unlock tongue-in-cheek achievements every session, presented like a video game ("Achievement unlocked"), and collect them all season.

- Race day is the main event; qualifying and practice get lighter treatment (a short carousel, Stories or a "Friday Report").
- One race carousel after the race, not one post per driver. The API allows 10 slides:
  1. Slide 1: cover teasing the biggest OOF of the day
  2. Slides 2 to 9: the eight juiciest drivers, biggest OOF first
  3. Slide 10: "The quiet ones" (drivers with Nothing to See Here)
- Rare moments (Grand Chelem, title clinched) get their own post.
- Every driver's full collection lives in a trophy cabinet page on the website; a Story can point there.
- Positive achievements go on a separate glory shelf, so the account stays about the OOFs.

**Season introduction**

- Three carousels during testing week, filling exactly one row of the profile grid before the first race.
- One driver per slide, teammates together; teams split 4/4/3, a cover plus 7 or 8 drivers per post (within the 10-slide limit).
- Order builds to the top: from the back of last season's constructors' standings to the front, the reigning champion as the very last slide.
- Poses: shared media-day classics (arms crossed, hands on hips, looking over the shoulder, leaning on the rear wing). No drivers' personal signature poses.
- Backdrop: a grey cardboard photo wall with a studio light visible at the edge.
- Each slide shows the full identity once: license plate with nickname, prop, motto as tagline, team colour.

## Season to season

The weekly loop repeats like the sport itself; each season stays fresh through things that build up, change and tell a story.

- **Seasonal achievement pack**: a few new achievements every season, like a game expansion (fresh ideas, rule changes, discoveries in the data). Retired ones become "vintage" in the trophy cabinets.
- **Report Card**: a mid-season post at the summer break.
- **The OOFies**: a gala-like awards show to close the season, like the real thing. Drivers wear tuxedos and the cardboard turns black and white.
- **Rivalries**: an occasional midweek post (Tuesday or Wednesday), e.g. teammate OOFscore battles or a close race at the top of the leaderboard.
- **Track dioramas**: a cardboard backdrop per circuit (e.g. Monaco's harbour, the Las Vegas Strip), improved each year. Each weekend opens with a preview post in which Rufus Oofley, the presenter character, presents the track layout.
- **Permanent marks** for continuity: Platinum achievements leave a lasting patch or strip of tape on the driver's box; the reigning champion races the next season with gold paint and a "1" on the license plate. Candidates in the same spirit: all-time trophy cabinets, a career OOFscore, milestones ("100th Lapped Dog"), a Hall of Fame / Hall of Shame.
- Not now: predictions.
- Still open: collections (completing a family of achievements unlocks a meta badge, e.g. "Permanent Record" for the school family).
- **Evolution post** (between seasons): young drivers get a two-stage nickname designed upfront and "evolve" to the second one, e.g. RafaCut becomes Rafined, with the prop changing along (rough diamond to polished). The moment is an editorial choice in the off-season, backed by something real from their season (first podium, strong first season). Framing in our own visual language, e.g. a school-family graduation with a diploma, not anything resembling Pokémon. Teased first: "RafaCut → ?" with a dark silhouette of the new model (only the outline of the diamond showing) and a caption inviting guesses; the reveal of the new model with the shiny diamond follows a day or two later. Several evolutions in one winter each get their own teaser and reveal, spread over the off-season. Later option: the reveal as a Reel (the diamond being polished).
- **OOF Classics**: an off-season series, one historic race per post, in that era's film look, with the achievements the race would have unlocked.
- **This week in OOF history**: a weekly midweek post with a fixed slot (Tuesday or Wednesday), picked from moments that happened in that calendar week, so there's always something to choose from. Mostly in-season, so sporadic per moment; with a career-long ambition, it needs a huge pile of historic events (see Historic moments archive).

**Weekend preview format (Rufus Oofley)**

- Slide 1, the round: Rufus on the right, the text on the left as an on-screen quote (track name, location, date). Every character faces left, so Rufus faces his own text. A small speech bubble is optional, only for a short opening line ("Welcome to Monaco!").
- Slide 2, the track: the circuit layout as a cardboard or paper diorama, with Rufus's head in a circle in the bottom-right corner (a broadcast picture-in-picture inset) and an info card: laps, length, corners, and one OOF stat from our own data (e.g. "Last year: 6 drivers lapped").
- Optional slide 3, "Last time here": the top three OOFs from last year's race at this circuit.
- Track shapes must be accurate: AI generators invent plausible but wrong layouts. Use real circuit outlines (e.g. bacinger/f1-circuits on GitHub, MIT licence) as the source, rendered as cardboard by the pipeline or used as a strict reference. Made once per circuit, reused every year.
- Backgrounds still to be determined; one idea is the circuit's cardboard backdrop for slide 1 and a plain background for slide 2.

**Head avatars (picture-in-picture)**

Every character gets a standard head avatar: a circle crop of the helmet (or Rufus's TV) from their portrait, made once and reused. Uses: Rufus's inset on the track slide, a grid of drivers on one slide (starting grid, standings, "The quiet ones", Oofies nominees), rivalry posts. Helmets must be recognisable on their own in a small circle, since car props (GoldRush's podium, Smoothie's smoothie) disappear in a head crop: distinct personal colours, and helmet props help.

**Starting grid post (race day)**

- A separate post, one to two hours before the race, once the grid is official and penalties are applied.
- A seamless carousel: one long image cut into slides that line up, so swiping moves along the grid from P22 to P1. The pole sitter is the reveal on the last slide; a cover slide at the start, pit-lane starters at the back.
- Seen from above, running horizontally, two staggered columns with big position numbers, about three rows per slide.
- Each slot is a driver's head avatar in a circle, flipped to face right (the direction of travel). A flipped version becomes a standard part of every avatar.
- Stats per driver (e.g. OOFscore so far, best result from this grid position, most-collected achievement) go in the caption, not on the grid.
- To check when built: whether OpenF1's starting grid includes penalties and how early on race day it's available; late pit-lane starts come from race control. A quick CMS review before posting.
- Look (from the tests): the track surface is black hook-and-loop fabric (the same velcro as the visors), with white masking-tape grid boxes that open towards the back, white felt position numbers behind each box, and a white tape edge line along the top and bottom. Fine sandpaper was the alternative surface.
- Four drivers per slide, with big avatar circles: 22 drivers make six grid slides plus a cover, seven in total. Boxes clearly staggered; some straddle the edge between two slides, which makes the swipe feel continuous.
- Built by the pipeline: the generator only provides the surface texture; boxes, numbers, avatars and text are placed by the pipeline so they line up exactly across the slides.
- Last slide: the start/finish line in front of the front row, as a strip of black-and-white chequered paper or tape across the full width of the track, slightly raised.
- Seamless technique: one long canvas (7 slides of 1080 × 1350 = 7,560 × 1,350 px) with everything placed on it, then cut into exact slices. The surface texture needs flat, even lighting and must be seamless and large (or repeated with random offsets), so no seams or patterns show. Sources: CC0 textures from ambientCG or Poly Haven (check the licence per file), or a flatbed scan of real velcro or sandpaper.
- Under each slot: the driver's nickname on a masking-tape label, in Shantell Sans (tested at phone size: readable, including long names like BearlyBraking). No stat line on the grid itself; stats go in the caption.
- The racing number as a small white roundel with a black number, overlapping the top right edge of the avatar circle, like an old race-car number sticker, so it can't be confused with the big grid position number. Check that it never covers a helmet prop (Plasteri's plasters, BearlyBraking's ears).
- Each label stays fully within one slide, also where the stagger brings a slot close to the edge between slides.

**Backdrop prototype (Monaco, Midjourney)**

The favourite: Monte Carlo's buildings climbing the slope with the mountain behind, the old town on the Rock on the left, the harbour with a few yachts in the middle, and a strip of track with a barrier and a red and white kerb across the foreground (where a character stands). Clear layering: every row of buildings is its own cut-out layer with a shadow behind it.

Learnings: asking for "simpler" makes Midjourney zoom out and flatten the layers; this version is still too detailed, so the next step is to edit this exact image to reduce the detail while keeping the composition and layering (ChatGPT image editing, or Midjourney's Editor with Retexture). Midjourney's --sref didn't work; attaching an image as a reference does.

Template prompt (only the landmark, the framing and the palette change per circuit):

```
layered cardboard and paper diorama backdrop of Monaco, three clear depth layers: in the foreground a strip of race track with a low barrier and a red and white kerb, in the middle the blue harbour with five or six simple white yachts, in the background the cliffs and the old town with simplified pastel buildings, windows as small cut-outs, a pale blue sky, moderate detail, simple clean shapes, a limited palette of five or six colours, calm and uncluttered, handmade paper theatre set, soft even studio light, no people, no text --ar 4:5 --style raw --s 150 --no text, logos, people, cars
```

**Workflow that works (two steps):**

1. Midjourney for the composition and mood, with the template prompt above.
2. ChatGPT image editing to simplify that exact image into the OOF style: keep the composition, layout, colours and layers, but merge buildings into fewer larger blocks with few windows, keep at most four boats, simplify mountains, trees and palms into a few bold flat paper shapes, and keep the sky plain.

Result for Monaco: calm, bold layered paper that matches the characters' clean collectible style. Still to tweak: turn two or three building blocks back into taller, slim towers, since Monaco's identity is its density and height.

Result for Las Vegas (same template and workflow): a straight-on front view (an angled, dynamic view didn't fit), hotel towers as bold blocks with a few glowing windows, palm silhouettes, 1950s neon starburst signs on poles, the Sphere as a building with a grid of glowing dots, desert mountains on the horizon, and the track strip across the front. A calm dark base of deep navy and muted purple with only hot pink and warm gold as neon accents. Learnings: the Sphere reads as a moon when it floats or sits behind the mountains, so it must stand on the ground with its base hidden behind the towers; Midjourney turned "neon starbursts" into fireworks, which the ChatGPT step fixed. A final edit moved every hill layer behind the Sphere, so only the towers overlap it: the prototype is done.

Monaco and Las Vegas side by side confirm the template works across very different circuits: day and night, pastel and neon, but clearly the same set designer.

**Foreground standard (every backdrop):** the whole foreground, from the top of the barrier to the bottom edge, fills the bottom 15% of the image height. From top to bottom within it: a low barrier, the dark asphalt track surface, the red and white kerb near the bottom, and a thin strip of asphalt below the kerb to the bottom edge. Anything else (grass, walls) goes above the barrier. Always a straight-on front view, with the track parallel to the image edge. Characters stand on this strip, so a fixed ground line lets the pipeline place every character at the same position on every backdrop. Monaco's proportion is the reference.

**Decision: the track strip is one shared asset.** Getting generators to produce the exact foreground takes too many correction rounds (Suzuka: a kerb in the middle of the track, then the whole scene pushed down under a huge sky). Instead, one standard strip (barrier, asphalt, kerb near the bottom) is made once, or drawn by the pipeline, and laid over the bottom 15% of every backdrop. The ground line is then identical on every circuit, and the generators only need to make the scene above it; whatever they put at the very bottom is covered.

Suzuka (third prototype): the Ferris wheel standing on the ground behind cherry blossom trees and a simple grandstand, with green hills and a pale spring sky. Needed fixes in ChatGPT: a black glitch on a gondola, the kerb drawn in the middle of the track, and a foreground of about 30% instead of 15% (which led to the foreground standard). Las Vegas matches the 15% ground line, but its kerb sits right under the barrier; for full consistency it can move down to near the bottom.

**Rivalry triggers**: around 8 to 12 rivalry posts per season, story-driven. The pipeline proposes them when teammates are within a few points in the OOFscore, the leaderboard leader changes, a driver suddenly racks up OOFs over a few races, or the top of the leaderboard is close in the final races; you decide.

### Historic moments: what to dig for

**Sensitive weekends first:** weekends with fatal accidents or serious injuries are not banned but flagged. Candidates from those weekends still come up, marked sensitive with a short factual note, are never published automatically, and never go into comedic formats; they can be used respectfully (to celebrate or honour). A weekend can also be set to blocked, after which nothing from it surfaces. See Build status for the details.

## Achievements

There are 55 achievements in the engine (43 on the OOF shelf, 8 on the glory shelf, 4 for the Safety Car), plus 9 new ones for the 2027 season. Tiers are final, from the test run on 2023 to 2026 data.

Every achievement also needs a visual: one origami object as its artwork (see Achievement art under Visual style). The object must be foldable, read at thumbnail size, and not clash with another achievement's object. Backgrounds (direction, to test with the first origami objects): one paper-craft background per theme rather than per achievement: school achievements on a blackboard (Outside the Lines, Teacher's Stare, Sent to Your Room, Expelled, Whole Class Detention, Class Dismissed, Failed the Driving Test), dining on a checked tablecloth (Table for One, Fine Dining, Early Lunch, Home Before Dinner, Meatball Sub), dogs on a dog bed or lawn (Lapped Dog, Good Boy, Zoomies), road and traffic on a strip of road with markings (Extra Wide, Forgot to Indicate, Selective Vision, Queue Jumper, Line Dancing), and the glory shelf on a velvet trophy-cabinet shelf. Six to eight backgrounds in total; the backgrounds sit inside the medallion, behind the coloured paper object (see Badge design).

**Badge design (direction, 9 November 2026)**

- The core badge is a medallion: inside it the theme background and the origami object, around it a rim in the tier's colour (bronze, silver, gold, holographic for Platinum). The rim tells the tier at a glance, like video-game trophies.
- The shape tells the shelf: a round medallion for the OOF shelf, a shield for the glory shelf (an honour never looks like an OOF), and a shape of its own for the Safety Car's achievements (e.g. a rounded square, like a road sign).
- Because the rim carries the tier, the origami objects are folded from normal coloured paper (a pink pig, a green tortoise, a red traffic light), not from tier foil. This makes them more recognisable and varied, and replaces earlier notes in this doc about objects in bronze, silver, gold or holographic foil.
- Two versions: the medallion alone as the standard (small uses: "+N" chips, the starting grid, captions), and the medallion on a wooden plaque for showcase moments (the unlock slide, the trophy cabinets).

**Achievement stamps (chosen design, 10 November 2026; replaces the badge, shield and medallion direction above)**

Every achievement is a postage stamp, made of matte paper and card, cute and cartoony.

- **Structure**: a coloured outer stamp as the main body, with a zigzag edge; a thin cream inner stamp with its own small perforated edge, framing the window closely; a recessed, slightly portrait window (about 4:5, like a classic postage stamp; decided 11 November 2026, so every object aims to be roughly square to slightly tall) with a sky-blue back wall and a green floor (or the achievement's theme background) holding the origami object; a round postmark in dark ink overlapping the window's top right corner.
- **Rarity**: the colour of the outer stamp (matte copper for Bronze, light grey for Silver, mustard yellow for Gold, a calm patchwork of a few large pastel sections for Platinum) plus a neat, centred row of one to four small, slightly raised cream felt shapes above the window (punched holes were tried and dropped as too flat). Colour notes: Gold should be a deeper, warmer old gold or ochre rather than mustard, and Silver a cooler, slightly bluish grey; pearlescent card for Gold and Platinum is an option if the colours alone don't read as precious.
- **One size and shape for every stamp**, so they fit the stamp book. Categories differ subtly within the same format: the shape of the felt pieces (stars for OOF, crowns for glory) and the window's theme background (e.g. deep red for glory, asphalt for the Safety Car). The Safety Car stamp has no tiers: its body colour follows the Safety Car character (still to finalise), with three round amber felt lights like a light bar.
- **The postmark carries the Grand Prix and the date** where the achievement was earned, added by the pipeline (the stamp design stays the same, only the postmark changes). Generators never draw the text.
- **The stamp book**: every driver gets an album (per season and lifetime), cover in team colours with his nickname, one fixed space per achievement so empty spaces show what's still missing. Repeated achievements stack in their space with a count; tapping the stack shows every copy with its postmark (circuit and date), grouped by year. Glory stamps can be calculated back to 1950, so retired legends can have albums too; cheeky stamps exist from 2023 on.
- **The matchbox**: kept for special occasions, e.g. the Oofies, where each award is handed over in a little matchbox that slides open to reveal its stamp.
- **Production notes**: the window needs its shadow-box depth back (the reference had it, the latest test lost it), and each object keeps its origami folds (the latest test turned the dog into flat cut paper). Objects may get small dark eyes and a nose; decide for the whole set. Origami style (tested 11 November 2026 on the Lapped Dog and Teacher's Stare objects): real folded origami from thin, crisp paper, with sharp creases, faint lines of earlier folds, a subtle paper sheen and slight translucency at thin edges; never cut felt pieces, never googly eyes or plastic. Each object is made separately on a transparent background, and the pipeline stacks background, object, window shadow, stamp and postmark. When an object alone doesn't tell the joke, add a character in origami rather than a non-paper detail (the owl for Teacher's Stare). Backgrounds: ask ChatGPT for a plain, solid white background and remove it afterwards in the pipeline (white objects on white, like the Line Dancing shoes, need a check that their edges survive); asking for transparency sometimes gives a drawn checkerboard. Spelling rule (11 November 2026): all public text (captions, slides, website) uses American spelling; achievement names and nicknames avoid words that are spelled differently in British and American English. Framing rule for every object: centred, with an even empty margin of about 15 percent on all sides, and a compact shape with no long parts sticking out to one side, so every object fills its stamp window the same way (learned from the toll barrier, whose long arm ran to the edge). Metal rule (11 November 2026): solid metal objects don't convince as paper (the school bell looked like painted metal or thick card), so keep any metal part small (like the wallet's coins) or choose a paper-friendly version of the object; this matters for the slingshot, key, gear stick, compass and bronze bar. Paper texture: ask for visible construction (crease lines, double-folded edges, seams) and a smooth surface with fine grain, never a mottled or felt-like texture. Reserve objects, tested and worth reusing: a crafted origami glider folded from a corrected homework sheet with red check marks (11 November 2026, looks great but too mischievous for Class Dismissed), and a bindle on a yellow pencil (11 November 2026, striking but unclear without someone carrying it), and the cream dog with its head out of a light-blue car window, ears flapping, eyes squinting and tongue out (11 November 2026, fantastic; fits a joyride or lap-of-honor achievement). Background candidate (not a decision, 11 November 2026): a deep red window background, from ChatGPT's first fully assembled stamp (the guilty dog). Cream, brown and white objects stand out strongly on it and it gives the window a warm "room" feel, but red objects (the trophy rosette, the lifebuoy, the tulip) partly blend into it, and it competes with the theme backgrounds; it could suit one category, for example the glory shelf as red velvet. That test also confirmed the stamp structure and the framing rule work. Paper look (12 November 2026): smooth, chunky rounded volumes read as metal or plastic whatever their color; ask for flatter origami construction with layered flaps, crisp folded edges and the pale reverse side of two-toned paper showing at the folds. Color rule (12 November 2026): prefer soft, slightly muted colors over bold, saturated ones. Workflow: Midjourney for direction, ChatGPT for the definitive versions.

## Scoring and rarity

Only OOF achievements earn points; the sum is the season's OOFscore leaderboard.

| Tier | Points | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | 10 | Holding the Door (every weekend) |
| Silver | 25 | Good Boy, Perfect Ten |
| Gold | 50 | Released Into the Wild, Drive-Thru |
| Platinum | 100 | Expelled, Red Light, Green Light |

The tiers in the achievement tables are final, set by the test run on 2023 to 2026 data (by awards per race weekend: 1 or more Bronze, 0.25 or more Silver, 0.05 or more Gold, below that Platinum, with a few deliberate exceptions). Glory achievements show their tier but earn no points.

- Tiers come from a test run on 2023 to 2026 data, not from guesses.
- Slides can show rarity: "Lapped Dog · Bronze · unlocked 31 times this season".
- Penalties stack: points per instance, shown once on the slide with a counter (High Five ×2), season total in the trophy cabinet.
- Threshold achievements (blue flags, deleted laps) unlock once per race.
- Driver-specific variants: an optional variant name per achievement and driver, used only for widely known catchphrases. It counts as the same achievement. Example: MaxSettings's Outside the Lines becomes Off Course, with "Outside the Lines · MaxSettings edition" as subtitle.

**Cascading achievements**

- One event, one achievement: when several achievements come from the same event, only the most specific one counts and appears on the slide.
- Bumper Cars absorbs the incident note (It Was Like That When I Got There) and the time penalty; the slide shows the penalty size as detail, e.g. "Bumper Cars · 10 s".
- Other overlaps: Good Boy replaces Lapped Dog; Fine Dining replaces Table for One; Teacher's Stare replaces Outside the Lines; a High Five for track limits replaces both track-limit achievements; Expelled replaces Sent to Your Room; Released Into the Wild absorbs its time penalty ("Released Into the Wild · 5 s").
- Separate events still stack: a crash on lap 30 and being lapped both count.
- In the pipeline: a "supersedes" relation between achievements; after calculating, any achievement covered by a more specific one from the same event is removed.
- Later option: frequent, funny combinations can become their own rare combo achievement.

## Nicknames and mottos

Every driver gets one positive, one-word nickname and a motto; no parody team names, teams go by colour ("the orange garage").

**Rules**

1. Positive or neutral at its core: teasing a fan would say with a smile.
2. About racing or the name, never the person: no looks, nationality, family, private life or money.
3. Survives a good weekend: "MaxSettings wins!" works.
4. Conjures its own image, not the real name with a letter changed (RainLance passes, it's a rain dance).
5. Nothing tied to serious crashes or injuries.
6. Double-entendre check before launch.

## Visual style and poses

Drivers wear their car as a cardboard box with shoulder straps, rendered cartoony 3D with realistic textures and shot like tilt-shift toy photography.

**Character**

- Same for everyone: box shape, race suit, proportions, oversized papier-mâché helmet with closed visor and googly eyes (Lollipopman uses no eyes).
- Per driver: team colour, one helmet motif from the nickname, one signature prop (a must), race number on the box.
- Per post: googly-eye direction, one of the poses, temporary damage (tape, tears, dents) from achievements.
- Easter eggs only: a cardboard wind-up key for special moments; a bobbling helmet in future Reels.
- Stay clear of Mario Kart, Yoshi's Crafted World backgrounds and LittleBigPlanet.
- Real tilt-shift, not just background blur: tilted focus plane, foreground and background soft, slightly high angle. Characters generated sharp on a clean background; the pipeline applies blur and composition.

**Prop rules** (props must read at feed and grid size)

- Oversized, cartoony scale: roughly helmet-sized.
- A distinctive silhouette that doesn't clash with achievement props (e.g. no trophy cups, which belong to the celebration poses).
- One prop per driver as the standard, no extra details. A driver may have both a helmet prop and a car prop only when it makes sense, never forced: the helmet carries who you are (stable over the years, visible in avatars), the car prop carries the nickname (it can change when the nickname evolves). Both must read at 150 px. First case: MaxSettings, with the lion's mane on his helmet and the mixing panel on his car (13 November 2026).
- Always in the same spot on the box.
- Strong contrast against the team colour.
- Test: shrink the character to about 150 px wide; if you can't tell who it is, simplify the prop.
- Props attached to the car are held by two strips of beige masking tape that run from the prop onto the car body, so the tape visibly bridges both surfaces; a slightly lifted or torn end adds to the handmade feel.

**Achievement art**

- Every achievement gets its own artwork, made once and reused forever: an origami object, more refined than the cardboard characters, so the achievement reads as the reward. Later seasons may add unique scenes per driver; not now.
- Tier shown in the paper itself: bronze, silver or gold metallic foil origami paper; holographic or iridescent paper for platinum.
- Each achievement needs one concrete, foldable object as its symbol (e.g. a dog for Lapped Dog, a door for Sent to Your Room; an ear trumpet for Selective Hearing). Abstract achievements get a concrete symbol.
- The same artworks fill the trophy cabinets on the website: a shelf of origami badges per driver.
- Slide layout still open: one achievement per slide is the starting idea (origami badge as hero, the character in the matching pose, the nickname and text), with the rest in the caption.

**Style anchor (decided 18 November 2026)**

- The style standard is the majority style of the set: large, clean faceted planes with a few crisp folds, the paler reverse side of the paper at some edges, markings and patches as folded paper pieces (never smooth sticker-like discs), cut-out paper facial features, warm and slightly muted colours. Example image: the mascot dog in Did the Thing (the version approved on 18 November 2026).
- History: the Smile for the Camera cheetah was briefly chosen as the anchor, but its dense faceting turned out to be the outlier: at stamp size dense facets become noise, and a dog pushed to that density lost its calm. This matches the earlier Expelled lesson (too many creases rejected in favour of large clean planes with a few crisp folds).
- The cheetah's colour follows the golden yellow of Silver Lining (Smile for the Camera was recoloured to match on 18 November 2026); its spots stay folded octagons.
- To do: an outlier pass. Only images that clearly stand out from the standard get adjusted (first: Smile for the Camera and Silver Lining, toned down to the standard), changing only the paper construction and colour saturation, never the pose, expression, composition or objects.
- Whisker rule (18 November 2026): no whiskers, except where they carry the joke. Only the mouse has them (By a Whisker, Giant Slayer). Outlier to fix in the pass: the cats in Mirror Match have drawn whiskers.

**Image files (decided 17 November 2026)**

- Masters: ChatGPT's full-size final PNGs on a plain white background, kept outside the repo in a backed-up folder (for example iCloud Drive). Only finals are kept; the history of what was tried and dropped lives in this doc, not in old image files.
- Repo: an optimised, web-sized copy of each final (for example 1200 px, compressed to a few hundred KB) at `resources/images/origami/achievements/<shelf>/<achievement-name>.png`, so the server gets them with every deploy. To build: a command that makes these copies from the masters automatically.
- Shelf folders: race, qualifying, practice, glory, oof-shelf, safety-car. File names: lowercase with hyphens, no apostrophes (e.g. `all-dressed-up-nowhere-to-go.png`). Variants, if ever needed: `<achievement-name>--<label>.png`.
- `resources/images` follows Laravel's type folders (css, js, views, images). No git LFS and no archive folder.
- The white background is removed by the pipeline (transparency is a pipeline step, not done in the master), which also centres the object in the stamp window.

**How team colours are chosen**

1. Primary (the car) is the colour fans associate with the team, not necessarily the largest area of the real livery (e.g. Mercedes teal, not silver or black).
2. Secondary (the suit) is the team's second identity colour, with strong contrast against the primary.
3. Every team gets a unique pair. Where real liveries are close, one team's pair shifts.
4. Simplified, never copied: two flat colours, no sponsor patterns or livery designs.
5. Set once per season, after the launches or pre-season testing, and stored per team per season (season\_teams).
6. The helmet is the driver's own, and must contrast with the suit and not match the team colours too closely.

**Typography**

- Headlines and big expressive text (post titles like "Saturday Scrapbook", Rufus's quotes, cover slides): **Delicious Handrawn**.
- Labels, data and small text that must be read quickly (grid name labels, info cards, stat lines): **Shantell Sans** (semi-bold), designed as a legible handwriting font.
- Both are free for commercial use (Open Font Licence, Google Fonts) and share the same handmade feel. Chosen over Permanent Marker, which is overused.
- Nicknames are always written in CamelCase (BearlyBraking, not BEARLYBRAKING): easier to read and narrower than capitals.
- Direction for Rufus's slides (to test in the real layout): everything Rufus says in Delicious Handrawn (two lines at most), the facts (Grand Prix, circuit, round) and his sign-off in Shantell Sans.

* Teammates share the car and suit; helmet and prop make them individual.
* Check case by case when a personal helmet colour is close to the team's secondary colour (GoldRush: silver Mercedes suit, so his helmet became mostly gold with a silver stripe).

**The car**

- Folded cardboard, not paper origami: the faceted folds of origami with the material of a cardboard box.
- A recognisable F1 silhouette: pointed nose, front wing, sidepods, engine cover, rear wing, four wheels. Worn with shoulder straps around the driver's waist.
- Damage follows the shape: bent front wing, crumpled nose, rear wing hanging by a strip of tape.

**License plate**

- Hand-lettered cardboard plate on the front wing, showing the nickname, the race number and the code OOF where an EU plate shows the country.
- Rendered by the pipeline, not the image AI: characters are generated with a blank plate.
- Layers: blank plate, text, damage overlay on top (clean, scuffed, torn corner, extra tape, hanging by one piece of tape), slight rotation or bend.
- Placement: the plate's four corner points stored once per pose; the pipeline warps the plate into that shape.
- Long names (HadTheAudacity, SoftLanding) need a wider plate, a smaller font or two lines.
- Fallback for season 1: the plate always stays clean, as a running gag.

**Season elements**

- One shared element on every car per season, made from household materials (e.g. an oversized AA battery for the electrical era, a wing flap on a split pin for active aero, a hand-drawn gauge).
- Only when the rules change visibly; a V8 return would mean a chunky engine block and toilet-roll exhausts.
- Every season: a registration sticker with the year on the plate, so every post is dated. 2027 likely only gets the sticker.

**Throwback posts (later)**

Throwbacks look like an old photo of a cardboard toy found in a shoebox: a film look that matches the era.

| Era | Look |
| --- | --- |
| 1950s | Black and white, coarse grain, soft contrast, faded print |
| 1960s | Black and white with deeper blacks, or early colour with muted, cool tones |
| 1970s | Warm, slightly faded colour, yellowish whites, strong grain |
| 1980s | Saturated colour, punchy reds and blues, finer grain |
| 1990s | Clean, vivid colour, fine grain |
| 2000s | Early digital: slight noise, blown highlights, cool cast |

- Pipeline: one colour LUT (.cube) per era plus a grain overlay, optionally a vignette or light leak; same composite principle as the tilt-shift.
- Period-accurate cars: cigar shapes in the 50s and 60s, tall airboxes and wide tyres in the 70s, turbo-era wedges in the 80s.
- Period-accurate helmets: e.g. open-face helmets with goggles in the early decades, full-face later.
- Data limits: older seasons only support results-based achievements (retirements, disqualifications, laps down, places lost, surprise wins); pit stops exist from 2011, lap times from 1996, race control messages from 2023.

**Image generation strategy**

- Start on the current ChatGPT plan for the master character sheet and the style; consistency, not volume, is the bottleneck.
- Upgrade to ChatGPT Pro ($100, 5x the Plus limits) for one month when producing all drivers and poses in bulk, then cancel.
- Later, once the style is fixed: the image API lets the pipeline generate variations itself (reserve drivers, damage states, season elements) at a price per image.

## Character bible

The reusable base model is not one image but a prompt in three layers: base spec (identical for every driver), driver spec, pose spec. Every image is generated from reference images plus all three layers, word for word, so details like the tape always come from the prompt, never from hand-editing. Small variation is fine as long as it follows the rules (it is handmade cardboard). The pipeline later does the same through the image API.

**How to generate an image**

1. Attach the approved base reference image (currently the latest Plasteri, with the round helmet and the dark charcoal eyebrows) and, once it exists, the driver's own approved image.
2. Paste the base spec, then the driver spec, then the pose spec.
3. Don't iterate endlessly on one image with "change only X": fix the wording in the bible instead and generate again.

**Layer 1: base spec (never changes per driver)**

```
Style: a polished 3D cartoon character, like a premium collectible
figure: clean, smooth surfaces, bold colours, soft even studio light.
Cute exaggerated proportions: a small chubby racing driver
with an oversized head.

Costume car: the driver stands upright on his own two feet and wears
a Formula One car made of new folded corrugated cardboard around his
waist, held up by two straps over his shoulders, like a homemade
costume. The car floats at waist height: its wheels do not touch the
ground, only his feet do. His legs and shoes are visible below it.
The car has a long pointed nose, a wide front wing, sidepods and a
rear wing. Smooth flat panels, crisp clean folds, corrugation visible
only at the cut edges. No tears, no wear.

Tyres: four wide black Formula One tyres made of folded matte black
cardboard in a low-poly style, like an early 3D video game model:
about 12 flat facets around the slick tread, crisp folds between the
facets, still round enough to read as wheels. Almost as wide as they
are tall, the rear tyres wider than the front. A thin plain white ring
on each sidewall. No text, no branding.

Helmet: an oversized, round racing helmet, like a big round dome:
the cute, stylised OOF helmet, not a realistic modern F1 helmet
with a long jaw. A broad, rounded chin bar with a flat underside,
never pointed, never oversized. A narrow, angular visor opening, tall enough for the
eyes. A small forehead vent and a small rounded cardboard visor pod on
the side. The back follows the curve of the helmet and ends in a
smooth rounded edge: nothing sticks out, no fins, no ears, no grooves.
Smooth matte papier-mache with only a faint hint of paper fibre,
no metal look, no crackle pattern.

Visor and face: the visor is covered in black hook-and-loop fabric
with a slightly fuzzy texture. Two big glossy googly eyes and two
eyebrows are stuck onto it. The eyebrows are simple straight
rectangular strips of thick, slightly raised felt in a very dark
charcoal (around #3A3A3A: only a little lighter than the black visor,
never grey). Their cut edges show a faint lighter rim where they catch
the studio light, and they cast a tiny shadow on the visor, so they
are visible against the black while clearly reading as dark eyebrows.
They are never curved: expressions come only from their height and
tilt.

Suit: a smooth satin-like race suit with a collar, cuffs, gloves and
shoes in an accent colour. No crackle or scale pattern.

Props on the car: attached with exactly two strips of beige masking
tape, each running from the prop onto the car body so the tape bridges
both surfaces, flat and pressed down. One end may be slightly lifted.
No tape floats in the air.

View: three-quarter view, the character faces the left of the image,
the car's nose points left.
Plain light grey background, soft shadow under his feet.
No text, no logos, no numbers, unless the driver spec asks for them.
```

### Data model (draft)

Raw first, then translate: API responses are stored as-is and translated into our own tables in a separate step, so parsing can be redone without fetching again. History from 1950 comes from a Jolpica database dump; the APIs keep the current season up to date. OpenF1 car telemetry and location data are skipped.

| Layer | Tables |
| --- | --- |
| Raw | raw\_responses (source, endpoint, parameters, session or round key, fetch time, JSON payload) |
| Core | seasons, circuits, rounds, sessions, drivers, teams, season\_teams, season\_drivers, driver\_entries, results, qualifying\_results, laps, pit\_stops, stints, positions, weather, race\_control\_messages |
| Events | events: one row per recognised incident (type, driver, session, lap, time, details, source message) |

- rounds (not "meetings" or "events"): season, round number, official and short name, the country the name refers to, a separate circuit ID, dates. Never derive a round's name or country from its circuit (e.g. the Bahrain Grand Prix held in Malaysia).
- "events" is reserved for the incidents layer.
- Race numbers live on driver\_entries (driver + team + number per session): champions' number 1, team-assigned numbers before 2014, per-race numbers in the 1950s and 60s, own numbers for FP1 rookies. drivers keeps a permanent number for reference only.
- season\_drivers is the roster: who is on the grid per season, for which team, usual number, race driver or reserve. Drives the season introduction, L-plates and default characters; a mid-season team switch gives two rows.
- teams is the lasting identity (Sauber to Audi, Toro Rosso to Racing Bulls); season\_teams holds the name and team colour per season.
- Identities: OpenF1 uses driver numbers and session and meeting keys, Jolpica uses text IDs and season plus round; drivers and rounds store both.

### Pipeline architecture

| Stage | What it does | Artisan command |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Ingest | Fetchers for Jolpica (dump + API) and OpenF1 store raw responses in raw\_responses | oof:import |
| 2. Normalize | Translators turn raw data into the core models | oof:normalize |
| 3. Excavate | One excavator per event type digs through the core models and produces candidates | oof:dig {type?} {--season=} |
| 4. Curate | Candidates are reviewed in the CMS; accepted ones become historic moments | (Filament) |

**Excavators**: an `Excavator` interface with `key()`, `version()` (bumped when the logic changes) and `dig(DigParameters)`, returning candidates. One class per event type in the catalogue, e.g. FewestStartersExcavator, GridToWinClimbExcavator, DroughtEndedExcavator. Each candidate carries a type, round, optional driver and team, a score and evidence (the numbers behind it, e.g. "only 6 starters"); the chaos score is computed from those scores to compare candidates across types.

| Table | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| excavation\_runs | Excavator, parameters, version, time and hit count per run: makes re-digging safe and traceable |
| moment\_candidates | What came out of the ground, status new / accepted / rejected / blocked; unique key (type, round, driver) prevents duplicates on re-dig |
| historic\_moments | The curated collection: date, round, title, story, tags, era. Separate from candidates, so a re-dig never overwrites decisions |
| moment\_sensitivities | Sensitive weekends (replaced the blocklist): round, optional driver, level (sensitive or blocked), severity (fatal, injury, minor) and a note; every excavator applies them |

The same pattern returns for the live achievements: excavators dig through history, detectors check every new session for achievements, on the same models. The CMS (Filament) is a few resources: candidates list with filters and approve and reject actions.

## Growth and launch strategy

**The principle: Reels bring people in, the series keeps them, the profile converts them.**

**Lesson from Bricklayin':** on the same account, image posts reached about one view each, Reels about 100. The account isn't held back; the format decides the reach. Carousels mainly reach people who already follow, Reels are how Instagram shows an account to people who don't. OOF is therefore Reels-first for growth, with carousels as the deeper content for followers. Bricklayin' stays the test lab: try Reel formats there first, at no risk to OOF.

### Platforms

| Platform | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Instagram | Home base: Reels for reach, carousels and stories for followers |
| Facebook | Mirror of Instagram, same posts |
| TikTok | The same vertical videos, unchanged. A large part of F1 fan culture lives there |
| YouTube Shorts | The same vertical videos, unchanged. Shorts are found through search, so titles matter. Long-form video (e.g. a season review with Rufus) is an option for later |
| X | Optional, later: where F1 conversation happens live |

Every Reel is made once and published to Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts.

### How every Reel is built

Every Reel follows the same structure, with the pay-off at the end:

1. **Hook (0 to 1.5 seconds):** a question or tension on screen before anyone decides to scroll on. "Who got sent to his room?" "Someone sat in the pit lane for 162 seconds."
2. **Build-up (the middle):** clues, a countdown or a slow reveal, so people keep watching to find out.
3. **Pay-off (the end):** the reveal, with the achievement unlocking and the signature chime.
4. **End card:** one line pointing to the series ("Every race weekend"), designed so the video loops back smoothly to the start.

Rules: 7 to 20 seconds; text on screen throughout (many people watch without sound), in the OOF fonts; the signature chime always lands on the pay-off, so it becomes OOF's recognisable sound.

### Reel formats

| Format | Hook | Build-up | Pay-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Achievement unlocked | The situation ("Lap 1, turn 1...") | A clue about who | The origami object folds open, the achievement unlocks, the driver is revealed |
| Guess the achievement | A silhouette and "Guess the achievement" | A short pause and a countdown | The reveal; viewers check their guess in the comments |
| Nickname reveal (one per driver) | A helmet head and a clue about the driver | The helmet turns | The nickname appears; made to be shared by that driver's fans |
| The weekend in 15 seconds | "The OOFs of the weekend" | A countdown through the biggest moments | Number one: the biggest OOF of the race |
| Starting grid | "Who starts on pole?" | A camera move along the grid from P22 forward | The front row, with Rufus at the start line |

Keeping the effort low: the backdrops are layered, so the pipeline can move the layers at different speeds for depth and a gentle camera move; short motion clips of origami objects and characters can be generated from the stills with AI video tools.

### Turning viewers into followers

- The profile sells the series: a bio that says in one line what OOF is, and three pinned posts (what OOF is, how the achievements work, the OOFscore).
- Seed the first signal: friends, F1 fans, F1 communities, early and funny replies to the big F1 accounts during race weekends. The first interactions matter disproportionately.
- Post fast: race Reels reach the most people while everyone's still talking about the race.
- Only original visuals (the cardboard characters, origami art, backdrops), which keeps OOF eligible for recommendation. Check Instagram's Account Status (Settings, Account Status) before launch and after the first posts.
- Measure per Reel: views, profile visits and follows. Watching without following means the profile isn't convincing yet. Review weekly, and learn from the best-performing Bricklayin' Reels (their first second, length, text on screen, sound).
